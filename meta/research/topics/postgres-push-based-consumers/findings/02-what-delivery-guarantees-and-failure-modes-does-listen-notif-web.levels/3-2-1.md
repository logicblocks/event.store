# On PostgreSQL 19, with the shared listener channel map committed in January 2026, how does throughput of transactions that INSERT and NOTIFY together compare with insert-only transactions at 10, 100 and 1000 concurrent connections?

## Question

On PostgreSQL 19, which includes the shared listener channel map committed in January 2026, how does the throughput of transactions that both INSERT and NOTIFY compare with insert-only transactions at 10, 100 and 1000 concurrent connections?

## Findings

### Short answer

No published benchmark makes this exact comparison: INSERT plus NOTIFY against insert-only, on PostgreSQL 19 code, at 10, 100 and 1000 concurrent writers. The PostgreSQL 19 release notes were still undated as of 14 September 2026. Primary sources do settle the shape of the answer, though:

- The January 2026 change does not touch the serialization point that separates notifying commits from ordinary ones.
- All the measurements that exist were taken on pre-19 code or on prototypes.
- Those measurements show the serialization point costing about half the throughput at 100 writers and about an order of magnitude at 1000.

### What PostgreSQL 19 changed and what it left alone

Commit 282b1cd, "Optimize LISTEN/NOTIFY via shared channel map and direct advancement", was written by Joel Jacobson and committed by Tom Lane on 15 January 2026. It adds a dynamic shared hash table (dshash) that maps each channel, identified by database OID and channel name, to the backends listening on it. It changes NOTIFY in two ways:

- **Selective wake-up.** A notifying backend now signals only the listeners that are interested in the channel.
- **Direct advancement.** When an uninterested listener is parked at the old queue head, its queue pointer is advanced past the new entries without waking it.

The PostgreSQL 19 release notes describe the change as "Improve NOTIFY to only wake up backends that are listening to specified notifications", adding that "Previously most backends were woken by NOTIFY."

The commit message limits the claimed benefit to one case. It reports "very significant improvements (integer multiples) in NOTIFY throughput ... when there are many listeners but only a few are interested in any specific message." It also says: "There is no improvement for the simplest case where every listener reads every message, but any loss seems below the noise level."

The commit keeps the cluster-wide heavyweight lock that NOTIFY takes at commit, and the new code depends on it:

- `async.c` on the REL_19_STABLE branch still calls `LockSharedObject(DatabaseRelationId, InvalidOid, 0, AccessExclusiveLock)` in `PreCommit_Notify`. The accompanying comment reads: "Serialize writers by acquiring a special lock that we hold till after commit. This ensures that queue entries appear in commit order, and in particular that there are never uncommitted queue entries ahead of committed ones".
- The direct-advancement logic is justified by that lock: "Because all writers serialize on a cluster-wide heavyweight lock, no other backend can insert entries between these two points."
- The file anticipates removing the lock: "if the heavyweight lock were ever removed for scalability reasons, we could achieve the same guarantee by holding NotifyQueueLock in EXCLUSIVE mode across all our insertions." That removal is not in PostgreSQL 19. The `async.c` history shows no later change to the locking, and the release notes contain no other NOTIFY item.

### Why INSERT plus NOTIFY diverges from insert-only

In `CommitTransaction()` on REL_19_STABLE, the steps run in this order:

1. `PreCommit_Notify()` takes the lock. It is placed late in pre-commit "to minimize time spent holding the notify-insertion lock".
2. `RecordTransactionCommit()` writes the commit record and, under synchronous commit, flushes WAL.
3. `ProcArrayEndTransaction()` runs.
4. `AtCommit_Notify()` signals listeners.
5. The lock is released with all other locks at `ResourceOwnerRelease`.

So every transaction that issued NOTIFY writes its commit record, waits for the WAL flush and signals listeners while holding one lock shared by the whole cluster. Notifying commits therefore complete one at a time. An insert-only transaction takes no such lock. Concurrent insert-only commits proceed in parallel and can share WAL flushes through group commit.

The following is an inference from the code ordering, not a measured result:

- **Notifying transactions:** adding connections only lengthens the queue for one lock. The commit rate is capped by the length of the serialized section, which includes a WAL flush when `synchronous_commit` is on.
- **Insert-only transactions:** throughput keeps rising with connection count until other limits are reached.
- **The gap:** it should widen as concurrency grows from 10 to 100 to 1000.
- **What PostgreSQL 19 changes here:** the channel map shortens the serialized section only by reducing signalling work. That helps mainly when many listening backends are not interested in the channel.

### Measured data points

All of these were taken on pre-19 code or prototypes, and most do not state the hardware.

- **INSERT plus NOTIFY at 100 clients.** On 4 September 2025, Rishu Bagga ran `pgbench -T 100 -c 100 -j 8` with a script of `BEGIN; INSERT INTO test VALUES(1); NOTIFY benchmark_channel, 'transaction_completed'; COMMIT;`.
  - Master gave 29,191 to 30,909 TPS.
  - A prototype gave 63,446 to 66,373 TPS, about twice as much. It moves notification data into WAL and holds the global lock only while writing a small fixed-size queue entry. It implements Tom Lane's proposal that the lock "only needs to be held for the duration of step 3, which is much shorter than what happens now".
  - At 100 writers, then, the current lock hold time costs at least half of the achievable INSERT plus NOTIFY throughput.
  - No insert-only baseline was reported, and neither hardware nor `synchronous_commit` was stated.
  - Reviewers then found assertion failures inside a critical section and failing tests. As of September 2025 the patch needed substantial rework, and it is not part of PostgreSQL 19.
- **NOTIFY at 1000 clients.** On 30 July 2025, Joel Jacobson ran one listening session against `pgbench -f notify.sql -c 1000 -j 8 -T 60`.
  - He got "tps = 7679 (with heavyweight lock; in commit order)" against "tps = 95430 (without heavyweight lock; not in commit order)", a 12.4x difference.
  - He concluded that the bottleneck "depends on the workload, which is either the kill() syscalls *or* the heavyweight lock".
  - With a single listener there is almost no signalling to save. This is the regime PostgreSQL 19 leaves unchanged.
- **NOTIFY at 8 clients.** In July 2025, Bagga measured about 158,000 to 190,000 TPS with the lock and about 299,000 to 354,000 TPS when skipping it, roughly 2x.
- **NOTIFY across concurrency levels.** On 23 July 2025, Jacobson measured on an Apple M3 Max:
  - Master peaked at 4 connections (177,351 TPS), then fell to 35,000 to 41,000 TPS from 16 to 128 connections.
  - His signalling patch at that point also peaked at 4 connections (250,910 TPS) and declined to 121,333 TPS at 128.
  - Throughput falls, rather than rises, beyond a handful of concurrent notifying connections.
- **10, 100 and 1000 listeners, not writers.** The most-cited 10, 100 and 1000 figures for this work count idle listening connections, not concurrent writers.
  - In a two-connection ping-pong benchmark, master fell from 9,126 TPS with no extra listeners to 6,233, 2,020 and 238 TPS with 10, 100 and 1000 extra listeners.
  - Early versions of the patch held about 8,900 to 9,350 TPS throughout.
  - In a separate run, with 1000 connections each listening on its own channel and notifying, throughput rose from 105 to about 3,100 TPS with the July v4 patch. That patch differs from the final committed design.
  - Listener count is the dimension PostgreSQL 19 fixes.
- **Production.** Recall.ai reported tens of thousands of concurrent writers each doing an INSERT and a NOTIFY in one transaction. They saw lock waits of over a second, hundreds of processes queued, and CPU and I/O usage dropping while query throughput collapsed. They took NOTIFY out of the write path. Public discussion of that incident contains no measured comparison of transactions with and without NOTIFY.

### Answer for PostgreSQL 19

On PostgreSQL 19, transactions that INSERT and NOTIFY still serialize their commits through one cluster-wide lock, held across the commit record and its WAL flush. Insert-only transactions do not. A reasonable expectation at each level:

- **10 connections:** a modest gap whose size depends on flush latency.
- **100 connections:** about half the achievable throughput lost. That is the only measured INSERT plus NOTIFY figure, and it is against a shorter-lock prototype rather than insert-only, so the gap to insert-only is probably larger.
- **1000 connections:** an order-of-magnitude penalty or worse, as queueing on the lock dominates.

The channel map in PostgreSQL 19 helps INSERT plus NOTIFY only when many backends are listening on other channels. In a design where every consumer listens on the same channel, the commit message itself predicts no improvement.

No independent benchmark of the released PostgreSQL 19 code covers this comparison. Anyone needing exact numbers must measure them on their own storage and `synchronous_commit` setting.
